Starbursting

Generate questions first, find answers later. This planning technique prevents blind spots and framing bias by forcing you to explore all angles before you start investigating.

"Better to ask the right questions than chase the wrong answers"
⚡ Why Starbursting?

The Problem: Most investigators jump straight into data collection, then realize halfway through they're missing critical information because they never asked the right questions. This creates blind spots and wasted time.

The Solution: Starbursting inverts the process. You generate ALL questions upfront using 6 categories (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How). Only after exhausting questions do you start collecting data. This prevents framing bias—the #1 cause of analytical failure.

The Result: A complete investigation roadmap before you touch any data. You know exactly what you're looking for, where to look, and how to validate findings. No surprises, no blind spots.

WHO?
  • Who is the primary target?
  • Who are associated actors?
  • Who created this content?
  • Who benefits from this information?
  • Who might have relevant data?
  • Who validates/contradicts findings?
?
WHAT?
  • What exactly are we investigating?
  • What data is public vs private?
  • What evidence validates conclusions?
  • What metadata is exploitable?
  • What biases might affect analysis?
  • What hypotheses should we test?
?
WHERE?
  • Where to search first? (platforms)
  • Where is data hosted? (jurisdiction)
  • Where is the target active online?
  • Where are archives stored?
  • Where can we cross-reference data?
  • Where are likely blind spots?
?
WHEN?
  • When is the deadline?
  • When did events occur?
  • When were accounts created?
  • When was content published/modified?
  • When to verify fast-changing data?
  • When to perform cross-validation?
?
WHY?
  • Why is this investigation necessary?
  • Why these specific objectives?
  • Why does the target behave this way?
  • Why is information hidden/public?
  • Why trust/distrust these sources?
  • Why this rigor level required?
?
HOW?
  • How to collect data? (techniques)
  • How to ensure legal compliance?
  • How to preserve OPSEC?
  • How to validate authenticity?
  • How to document chain of custody?
  • How to structure final report?
🔄 4-Step Workflow
1
Draw the Star (2 min)
Use whiteboard, mind map tool, or plain text. Put your subject in the center (one clear sentence), add six branches for Who/What/Where/When/Why/How.
2
Generate Questions (15-30 min)
Critical: Do NOT answer questions as they come up. Just generate. Aim for at least 3 questions per branch. The best questions often appear only after you've exhausted obvious ones.
3
Prioritize & Organize (10 min)
Mark questions as Critical (🔴), Important (🟠), Desirable (🟢), or Optional (⚪). Identify dependencies (which answers unlock others). Map to OSINT phases (Planning/Collection/Correlation/Analysis/Reporting).
4
Create Action Plan (15 min)
For each priority question: Can you answer immediately? Needs quick research? Requires deep investigation? Might be unanswerable (document why and impact)?
